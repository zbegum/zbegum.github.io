A Critical Review of Sketch Collection Methods: Remembering How Humans Really Sketch

E. Dede, Zeynep Begüm Kara, and T. M. Sezgin

Computers & Graphics (Elsevier), 2026

Paper

Do the sketch datasets capture how people actually sketch, or only what is easier to collect? Sketching is a natural way of expressing, processing, and communicating ideas — but as data needs for intelligent systems grow, sketch collection has often shifted toward what is easy to collect rather than what is ecologically valid. In this paper, we argue that sketch datasets are systematically shaped by their collection protocols and experimental conditions, and therefore should be reported transparently and interpreted in light of the conditions that produced them.

We structure this discussion around six contextual factors — time, tools, background, audience, multimodality, and elicitation. Through these factors, we analyze 86 sketch datasets from 2002–2025, suggest practices for collecting sketch data that better reflect the diversity of human sketching, and include sketches from our exploratory study to show how different collection conditions can lead to very different kinds of sketches.

LACY: Simulating Expert Mentoring for Software Onboarding with Code Tours

Zeynep Begüm Kara, A. İsmail, E. Ateş, İ. N. Tamcı, Z. İyigün, S. Ş. Aslangül, Ö. Devran, B. M. Uçar, and E. Tüzün

34th ACM International Conference on the Foundations of Software Engineering (FSE 2026), Industry Track

Paper Code

Onboarding new developers into legacy codebases is one of the most time-consuming responsibilities in software organizations, often relying on senior developers to provide one-on-one mentorship that does not scale. LACY, developed in collaboration with Beko, is a hybrid human–AI VS Code extension that captures expert mentoring as reusable code tours. The system combines AI-generated explanations with expert curation, integrating interactive walkthroughs, contextual quizzes, and narrated podcasts so that expert knowledge can be authored once and transferred across an organization asynchronously.

Narrative Tension Field: Reading a Book as Geometry

MIT Summer Geometry Initiative (SGI), 2026 · SGI 2026 Fellow

Code Figures

One of my projects at MIT SGI. This project, mentored by Congyue Deng, explores how the semantic structure of a story can be turned into readable geometric visualizations. The approach treats a novel as a route through its own meaning space, placed on a surface that captures its emotional tone. The goal is to show how a story’s ideas, mood and reading order change together, using geometry processing to make narrative structure easier to see.

Message in the Codons: A Working Protein, A Hidden Message

MIT How to Grow (Almost) Anything (HTGAA), 2026

Tool Coursework Profile

My individual final project for MIT’s How to Grow (Almost) Anything (HTGAA) 2026, which I took as a Committed Listener. The genetic code has 64 codons but only 20 amino acids, so many amino acids can be written with several synonymous codons. This tool hides a message in those choices: the text is Huffman-compressed into a bit stream, and each bit picks a codon while the protein stays the same. The encoded mScarlet3 or sfGFP still works and glows, and the message can be decoded back from the DNA.

A Case Study on Gemini's MCQ Success: Defining Directed Multiple-Choice Questions

Individual Research, Bilkent University, 2023–2024

Slides

Individual Research under supervision of Prof. H. Altay Guvenir I designed a Directed MCQ (DMCQ) system to evaluate how Large Language Models like Gemini perform under human-informed guidance. The study simulates collaborative test-solving scenarios, where human cues and AI analysis jointly contribute to the elimination of options. The goal is to understand how such directed interaction influences model reasoning and accuracy in structured evaluation settings.

A Literary Approach to Computer Music: Translation of Words

Hobby Project, 2022

Report

This project aims to generate computer music by transforming input text, specifically its rhythmic and harmonic aspects, into synthesized instrument sounds. The core idea is to correlate language and music by analyzing language syntax through rhythm and phonetics through harmony and sound combinations. The project creates two-channel music, with one channel producing drum sounds from individual letters (vowels, consonants, spaces) and the other generating piano chords from grouped syllables, offering an experimental exploration of how diverse texts translate into musical compositions using computers , drawing inspiration from historical rhythmic structures such as the dactylic hexameter of the Iliad and Arabic prosody (Aruz ölçüsü).
